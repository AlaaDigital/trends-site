Strait of Hormuz Oil Flow Rebounds to 80% as Iran Chokehold Breaks Down

Middle East oil exports rebound to near 80% of pre-war levels as Iran's ability to choke Strait of Hormuz breaks down. Saudi Arabia's crucial East-West pipeline restarts Yanbu exports, while Brent jumps to $106.92 after Trump rejects Iran deal.

1. Oil Exports Rebound - 80% Recovery

Middle East Oil Exports Rebound as Iran's Chokehold on Hormuz Breaks Down — that's the headline from The Wall Street Journal today. DUBAI—Iran's ability to choke off oil flowing through the Strait of Hormuz—and use that as leverage in talks with the U.S.—is breaking down, raising the risk it will resort to military escalation to bolster its position.

The erosion of Iran's position comes as the U.S. Navy and Gulf oil producers have become better at fending off or evading Iranian attacks, allowing more tankers to cross the strait. Shipments via Hormuz and bypass routes were delivering just under 80% of their prewar regional flows as of last week, according to tracker Kpler.

Analysts note Middle Eastern crude exports rebounded this month to around their highest level since the war began in February, but Strait of Hormuz flows remain far below the pre-war 17,133 kilobarrels a day baseline.

2. Saudi Arabia's Bypass - East-West Pipeline Restarts

The recovery is powered by Saudi Arabia's crucial bypass route. Saudi Arabia has resumed oil loadings from its Red Sea port of Yanbu after restarting operations on the East-West Pipeline, according to trade sources and shipping data.

Drone attacks, which Saudi Arabia blamed on Iraqi militias, forced the kingdom to shut the pipeline on September 11, halting crude exports from Yanbu. Saudi Arabia has resumed exporting oil via its East-West pipeline after repairing the damage caused by drone strikes earlier this month, restoring partial flows on a major route that bypasses the Strait of Hormuz.

State energy firm Saudi Aramco was testing the pipeline last week and building pressure, aiming to resume meaningful flows by the weekend, sources said.

3. Oil Prices Jump - Trump Rejects Iran Proposal

Price reaction was immediate. Brent futures rose $2.60, or 2.49%, to $106.92. West Texas Intermediate gained $2.08, or 2.25%, to $94.49. The move followed President Donald Trump's rejection of an Iranian proposal to resolve the Strait of Hormuz conflict and reopen the waterway to shipping.

Brent crude futures rose 1.5% to $106.9 a barrel, while US West Texas Intermediate crude gained 1.5% to $93.9 a barrel in later trading, with traders pricing in elevated geopolitical risk premium.

"The U.S. Navy blockade has locked out Iran's ports and caused its trade to free fall, while Gulf producers learned to evade attacks" — WSJ analysis

What It Means

Strait of Hormuz oil flow is now the most critical keyword for energy markets. With 20% of world's oil passing through this chokepoint, the shift from Iranian blockade to 80% recovery via alternative routes (Yanbu, East-West pipeline) changes negotiating leverage. Trump's rejection keeps risk premium high, but Saudi bypass reduces worst-case scenario.

If you track energy, watch Kpler tanker data and Yanbu loadings — they are the real-time indicator of Hormuz bypass success.
