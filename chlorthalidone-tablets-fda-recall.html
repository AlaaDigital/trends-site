Chlorthalidone Tablets FDA Recall: 13,567 Bottles Pulled for Dissolution Failure

If you take blood pressure medication, check your bottle. The FDA just classified a nationwide recall of generic Chlorthalidone 25mg tablets as Class II after the pills failed to dissolve properly — which could make them less effective.

Quick check: This recall affects Inventia Healthcare Limited Chlorthalidone Tablets, USP 25mg, 100-count bottles, Lot RISA24002 (expiry April 2027). NDC 64980-599-01. FDA Recall Numbers D-0610-2026 and D-0852-2026. If your bottle matches, contact your pharmacist — don't stop on your own.

What exactly was recalled?

According to the FDA Enforcement Report, Inventia Healthcare Limited, an India-based manufacturer, initiated a voluntary recall on September 3, 2026 for 13,567 bottles of Chlorthalidone tablets, USP 25mg. The FDA officially classified it as a Class II recall on September 17, 2026.

Why failed dissolution matters

Chlorthalidone is a thiazide-like diuretic — a "water pill" — that helps your kidneys remove salt and water to lower blood pressure. For it to work, the tablet must dissolve at a specific rate.

When a tablet fails dissolution testing, it doesn't automatically mean it's toxic. It means the lab test showed the drug didn't release its active ingredient as expected. In real life, that can mean you get less medicine than prescribed, which for blood pressure control can be clinically important. Blood pressure doesn't have symptoms — you might not feel that it's not working.

What does Class II mean? Should you panic?

The FDA uses three levels:

  • Class I: Dangerous — could cause serious harm or death.
  • Class II: Remote risk of serious harm, but may cause temporary or reversible issues. This is where Chlorthalidone falls.
  • Class III: Not likely to cause harm, but violates manufacturing standards.

So Class II is not a "stop immediately" emergency, but you shouldn't ignore it. The FDA itself says for Class II and III: "consumers can generally continue taking the medicine unless the recalling company provides other instructions. In some instances, stopping your medicine may be more harmful than continuing."

What to do: 1) Check your bottle label for NDC, lot, and manufacturer Inventia Healthcare. 2) Call your pharmacy — they can confirm in 30 seconds. 3) Do NOT stop or switch dose on your own. 4) If your bottle is affected, your pharmacist will arrange a replacement lot.

This is the second recall in 3 months

This is not the first time. On June 5, 2026, Inventia recalled 11,460 bottles of the same drug for the same reason — failed dissolution. That means over 25,000 bottles have been pulled nationwide since summer. The batches are different, but the root cause is identical, which is why the FDA is watching closely.

How to check your medication at home

Take your bottle and look for:

  • Does it say Chlorthalidone Tablets, USP 25 mg and Manufactured by Inventia Healthcare Limited?
  • Is the NDC 64980-599-01?
  • Is the lot RISA24001 / RISB24002 / RISA24002?

If yes to all, it's part of the recall. Call your pharmacy today. Bring your bottle with you — they will need lot and expiry.

"Don't stop taking prescribed blood pressure medication without talking to your doctor or pharmacist. Sudden stops can be riskier than the recall itself." — American Heart Association guidance
